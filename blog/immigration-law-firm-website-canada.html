Website design for immigration law firms in Canada: what turns visitors into consultations

People looking for immigration help are often anxious, short on time and comparing several firms at once. Your website has a few seconds to show that you understand their situation and can be trusted. Here is what we have learned designing for immigration practices in Canada, including the website for A&M Canadian Immigration Law Corporation in Winnipeg.

Organise the site around pathways, not departments

Visitors think in terms of their own goal: "I want to study in Canada", "my application was refused", "I want to sponsor my spouse". Build your navigation around those goals:

  • Permanent residence: Express Entry, Provincial Nominee Programs and family sponsorship.
  • Temporary residence: study permits, work permits, LMIA and visitor visas.
  • Urgent matters: refusals, procedural fairness letters, inadmissibility and appeals.
  • Citizenship, and services for US residents moving to Canada.

Each pathway page should explain who it is for, the main requirements in plain language, typical next steps and how your firm helps, with a clear button to book a consultation.

Show credentials early

Trust is everything in this field. Make credentials easy to find on every page:

  • The lawyer's name, photo, call to the bar and Law Society membership, or the consultant's College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants licence number.
  • Years of practice, languages spoken and professional associations.
  • Real Google reviews, displayed with permission and linked to the source.

Keep marketing claims within your regulator's rules. Law societies and the College set rules on advertising, so avoid guarantees about outcomes.

Publish content people search for every week

Immigration rules and draw results change constantly, and people search for updates. A news section with draw results, policy changes and short explainers gives visitors a reason to return and gives search engines fresh pages to index. On the A&M website we designed live Manitoba PNP draw tables and illustrated roadmaps that explain complex pathways at a glance. See the A&M case study.

Make booking effortless

  • Put a "Book a consultation" button in the header and at the end of every page.
  • Let visitors pick a time online, with clear information about consultation fees.
  • Offer a short intake form so you know the case type before the call.
  • Show office location and hours, plus remote consultation options for clients outside your city.

Reach clients in their own language

Many clients are more comfortable reading in their first language. Even a few key pages translated into the languages your team speaks can lift enquiries, and it signals that you understand your community.

Technical basics that protect your rankings

  • Fast loading on mobile phones, where most visitors arrive.
  • Clear page titles such as "Express Entry Lawyer in Winnipeg".
  • Accessible design that meets WCAG 2.0 Level AA, which also helps with Ontario's AODA rules.
  • Structured data for your firm and FAQs so search engines and AI assistants can understand your services.

FAQ

What pages should an immigration law firm website have?

At minimum: a home page, one page per main pathway (Express Entry, PNP, study and work permits, family sponsorship, refusals and appeals), a lawyer or team page with credentials, a news section, reviews, and a contact and booking page.

Can an immigration website give legal advice?

Websites should share general information and invite visitors to book a consultation. Personal advice belongs in a consultation, and marketing must follow your regulator's rules.
