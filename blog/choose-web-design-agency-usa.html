How to choose a web design agency in the USA: 12 questions to ask

Hiring a web design agency is a big decision. The right partner brings in customers for years. The wrong one leaves you with a site you cannot update, a missed deadline and an invoice you did not expect. These twelve questions help US business owners compare agencies on what matters.

Questions about results

  • 1. What business problem will the website solve? A good agency asks about your customers and goals before it talks about colours and fonts.
  • 2. Can you show similar work? Look for projects in your industry or with a similar goal, such as lead generation, e-commerce or a SaaS product. Ask what changed for that client after launch.
  • 3. How will we measure success? Agree on a few clear numbers, such as enquiries, booked calls, sales or sign-ups, and set up analytics to track them from day one.

Questions about the process

  • 4. What does your process look like, week by week? You want a clear sequence: discovery, structure and wireframes, design, development, testing and launch, with review points where your feedback lands early.
  • 5. Who will actually work on my project? Ask whether the team is in-house, freelance or overseas, and who your day-to-day contact is.
  • 6. How do you handle feedback and changes? Find out how many design rounds are included and how extra requests are priced.

Questions about cost and ownership

  • 7. What exactly is included in the price? Copywriting, photography, SEO set-up, accessibility work, hosting, training and post-launch support are often not included unless they are written down.
  • 8. Who owns the design files, code and domain? You should. Make sure the contract transfers ownership on final payment and that the domain is registered in your name.
  • 9. What will it cost to run the site after launch? Ask about hosting, platform subscriptions, plugin licences and maintenance plans.

Questions about quality

  • 10. How do you make sites fast? Ask how they handle images, video and code, and how they test speed on mobile. Google's PageSpeed Insights is a quick, free check.
  • 11. How do you handle accessibility? In the US, website accessibility is an ADA risk area. Agencies should design and test against WCAG 2.1 Level AA. Our accessibility guide explains the basics.
  • 12. What happens if something breaks after launch? Look for a warranty period and a clear support option.

Local, offshore or distributed teams

Many US businesses now work with distributed agencies: a point of contact in North America and part of the design or development team in another country. Done well, this gives you senior skills at a fairer price and work that continues around the clock. Done badly, it means slow replies and miscommunication. Ask about overlapping working hours, response times and how progress is reported each week.

Red flags to watch for

  • A fixed price before anyone has asked about your goals.
  • No portfolio pieces with live links or real client names.
  • Vague answers about who owns the work.
  • Promises of guaranteed first-page Google rankings.
  • No written scope or timeline.

FAQ

How much does a web design agency charge in the USA?

Prices vary widely with scope. A custom small-business website often costs several thousand to a few tens of thousands of US dollars, while e-commerce sites and web apps cost more. Always compare written scopes, not just totals.

Is it safe to hire an agency with an overseas team?

Yes, if the agency has a clear process, a North American point of contact, a written contract that gives you ownership of the work, and regular progress updates.
