Social media marketing for small businesses in Canada and the USA: what to post and how often

Most small business owners know they should be on social media, but not what to post, where, or how often. This guide gives you a simple system that works for local businesses and service companies in Canada and the USA, based on the campaigns we run for clients such as Funpark Indoor Amusement Center.

Pick the right platforms

You do not need to be everywhere. Choose one or two platforms where your customers already spend time:

  • Instagram and Facebook: strong for local businesses, family activities, restaurants, retail and events.
  • LinkedIn: best for B2B services, professional firms and recruiting.
  • TikTok and YouTube Shorts: strong for products, entertainment and businesses that can show something visual quickly.
  • Google Business Profile posts: often overlooked, but seen by people already searching for you.

Use content pillars

Content pillars are three to five recurring themes that make planning easy. For example:

  • Offers: a weekly deal or seasonal promotion, like Funpark's "Discount Tuesday".
  • Proof: reviews, results, before-and-after work and case studies.
  • Behind the scenes: your team, your process and how things are made.
  • Education: quick tips and answers to common customer questions.
  • Moments: holidays, local events and seasonal posts.

How often should you post?

Consistency matters more than volume. A realistic starting point for most small businesses is three to five feed posts per week on your main platform, plus short stories or reels when you have something worth showing. Pick a schedule your team can keep for months, then increase it if results justify it.

What makes a post work

  • One clear idea per post. Big, readable text and a single message.
  • Real people and places. Your own photos and videos beat stock images.
  • A reason to act. Book now, visit this week, comment, share or save.
  • Native formats. Vertical video for reels and shorts, square or portrait images for feeds.
  • Brand consistency. The same colours, fonts and tone, so people recognise you before they read your name.

Plan a month at a time

Build a simple calendar with your pillars, key dates such as Canada Day, July 4th, Thanksgiving in both countries, Halloween and the winter holidays, and any launches or offers. Create posts in batches and schedule them, so social media takes a few hours a month instead of a few hours a day.

Add a small paid budget

Organic reach on most platforms is limited. Boosting your best posts or running small, targeted ads to people in your area can multiply results. Start small, test two or three versions, and keep what works.

Measure what matters

Likes are nice, but focus on actions: messages, calls, bookings, website visits and sales. Use tracked links and ask new customers how they found you.

Know the rules

In Canada, CASL rules apply to commercial electronic messages, so get consent before sending marketing emails or similar direct messages. In the USA, paid partnerships with influencers must be clearly disclosed under FTC guidelines.

FAQ

Should I hire an agency or post myself?

Many small businesses post day-to-day updates themselves and use an agency for strategy, templates, campaign design and video. A good template system lets your team publish on-brand posts quickly.

How long until social media brings customers?

Expect a few months of consistent posting to build an audience. Paid promotion can bring faster results while your organic presence grows.
