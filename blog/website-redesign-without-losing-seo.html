Website redesign checklist: how to redesign without losing your Google rankings

A new website should bring more customers, not fewer. Yet many businesses in Canada and the USA see their search traffic drop after a redesign because old pages disappear, addresses change or important content is removed. This checklist helps you keep the rankings you have earned while you upgrade.

Before the redesign

  • Record your baseline. Export your top pages, search queries and traffic from Google Search Console and your analytics tool, so you can compare after launch.
  • Crawl your current site. Use a crawler to list every live URL, page title, meta description and heading.
  • Find your most valuable pages. Identify the pages that bring the most traffic, links and enquiries. They need the most care.
  • Decide what stays, merges or goes. Keep pages that perform well, combine thin or duplicate pages, and only remove pages that have no value.

Plan your URLs and redirects

This is the step that protects your rankings.

  • Keep the same URLs wherever possible.
  • When an address must change, map every old URL to the most relevant new URL.
  • Set up permanent 301 redirects for every changed or removed page. Avoid sending everything to the home page.
  • Avoid redirect chains, where one redirect leads to another.

Protect your content

  • Keep the substance of pages that rank well. Improving them is fine, but deleting key sections often costs rankings.
  • Keep or improve page titles, headings and meta descriptions.
  • Keep internal links between related pages, and update them to the new addresses.
  • Keep structured data, such as business details, FAQs and article markup.

Build and test on a staging site

  • Block search engines from the staging site with a noindex tag or password, so it is not indexed by mistake.
  • Test every redirect from your URL map.
  • Check speed on mobile with Google PageSpeed Insights.
  • Check accessibility, forms, booking, tracking and analytics.

Launch day

  • Remove the noindex tag or password from the new site. This is the most common and most damaging launch mistake.
  • Make sure the robots.txt file allows search engines to crawl the site.
  • Submit your new XML sitemap in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.
  • Test the top 20 old URLs to confirm they redirect correctly.

After launch

  • Watch Search Console daily for the first two weeks for crawl errors and 404 pages, and fix them with redirects.
  • Compare traffic and rankings with your baseline. Small dips for a few weeks are normal; large lasting drops point to missing pages or redirects.
  • Keep your old redirects in place for the long term.

Changing domain at the same time

If you are also moving to a new domain, redirect every old page to its matching new page, keep the old domain registered, and use the change of address tool in Google Search Console.

FAQ

Will my rankings drop after a redesign?

A short, small fluctuation is common. With careful redirects, preserved content and a clean launch, most sites recover quickly and often improve.

What is a 301 redirect?

A permanent redirect that sends visitors and search engines from an old URL to a new one, and passes most of the old page's ranking value to the new page.

How long should I keep redirects?

Keep them for at least a year, and ideally permanently, especially for pages with backlinks.
