Local SEO for small businesses in Canada and the USA: a practical guide

When someone searches "accountant near me", "immigration lawyer Winnipeg" or "web designer Toronto", Google shows a map with three businesses at the top. Getting into that list is called local SEO, and for most small businesses in Canada and the USA it is the fastest way to win customers from search. This guide covers the steps that matter most.

How Google ranks local results

Google says local results are based mainly on three things:

  • Relevance: how well your business profile and website match what the person searched for.
  • Distance: how close your business is to the searcher or the location in their search.
  • Prominence: how well known your business is, based on reviews, links, mentions and your overall web presence.

You cannot change distance, but you can work hard on relevance and prominence.

Step 1: Claim and complete your Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile is free and is the single most important local SEO asset.

  • Choose the most accurate primary category, such as "Immigration attorney" or "Website designer", then add secondary categories.
  • If you serve clients at their location and do not have a storefront, set it up as a service-area business and list the cities you serve.
  • Add your hours, phone number, website, services, products and a clear description.
  • Upload real photos of your team, office and work, and add new ones regularly.
  • Post updates and offers. They show that the business is active.

Step 2: Keep your name, address and phone number consistent

Your business name, address and phone number (often called NAP) should be exactly the same on your website, Google, Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, Facebook, Yelp and industry directories. Inconsistent details confuse search engines and customers.

Step 3: Build local pages on your website

  • Put your city and region in page titles and headings where it is natural, for example "Branding Agency in Calgary".
  • If you serve several cities, create a genuinely useful page for each, with local examples, not copies with the city name swapped.
  • Add your address and a map to your contact page, and use structured data so search engines understand your business details.

Step 4: Earn reviews the right way

Reviews influence both rankings and decisions. Ask every happy client for an honest review, make it easy with a direct link, and reply to every review, good or bad.

Do not buy reviews, write fake ones or offer rewards for positive reviews. Google's policies prohibit it, the US Federal Trade Commission has rules against fake reviews, and Canada's Competition Bureau has acted against misleading reviews.

Step 5: Get mentioned locally

Links and mentions from local sources build prominence. Join your chamber of commerce, sponsor community events, partner with complementary businesses, and get listed in reputable directories for your industry.

Step 6: Make your website fast and mobile-friendly

Most local searches happen on phones. A slow or confusing mobile site loses customers even if you rank well. Put your phone number as a tap-to-call link, show your service area clearly and make booking simple.

Track what matters

Google Business Profile shows how many people called you, asked for directions or visited your website. Combine that with Google Search Console and analytics to see which pages and searches bring enquiries.

FAQ

How long does local SEO take to work?

Completing your Google Business Profile can make a difference within weeks. Building reviews, local pages and links is ongoing work that usually shows steady gains over several months.

Can I rank in a city where I do not have an office?

Distance matters, so it is harder. A service-area profile, strong local pages and real local work and reviews give you the best chance.

Do I need both Google and Bing listings?

Yes. Bing powers several search and AI assistant experiences, and setting up Bing Places takes only a few minutes.
